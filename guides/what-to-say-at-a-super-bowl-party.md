**The short version:** pick a team before you arrive, cheer when the room cheers, groan when it groans, and keep a few safe lines in your pocket. Nobody at a Super Bowl party is quizzing you. Half the room is there for the food and the commercials too. They're just better at hiding it.

## Before you go: pick a side

The question you'll get most is "Who are you rooting for?" Have an answer. The easy picks:

- **The team your host is rooting for.** You're a guest. This is just manners.
- **The underdog.** Everyone likes an underdog, and it never needs explaining.
- **The team with the better story.** Look at one headline the morning of the game. Is one team's quarterback young? Is a coach retiring? Is one team there for the first time in years? That's your reason.

> "I'm going with the underdog. I like a story."

## Lines that work all night

These are true in almost any game, so you can say them with confidence.

> "This is a big drive."

Say it any time the team you picked has the ball and the room gets quiet.

> "Defense has to step up here."

Say it when the other team has the ball and is getting close.

> "That's a huge call."

Say it when a referee throws a yellow flag and half the room yells at the TV.

> "They need to get the ball back with time left."

Say it in the fourth (last) quarter if your team is behind.

## What to ask (and not sound lost)

Asking is fine if the question sounds like you're curious, not confused.

- "Who's been the best player for them this season?" Then just listen. People love this one.
- "Do you think they go for it?" Use it when the room is debating the next play. It almost always fits.
- "Was that a good call?" Only after a flag. Then agree with whoever answers loudest.

## When to cheer

Follow the room, about half a second behind. The big moments are a **touchdown** (6 points, loud cheering, people stand up), a **field goal** (3 points, polite clapping) and an **interception or fumble** (the ball changes teams, so one half of the room is thrilled and the other half is upset). If you're not sure which half you're in, check your host's face.

## Halftime and the commercials

You're off the hook. Halftime is the concert, and the commercials are a legitimate topic. "Which ad was your favorite?" is the single safest question of the night, and you can ask it to anyone.

## What not to say

- **"Who's playing?"** Look it up in the car.
- **"Why do they keep stopping?"** Everyone wonders this. Nobody says it.
- **Anything about "sportsball."** It's funny once, to you, at home.
- **Calling a team by the wrong city.** If you're not sure, use the team name ("the Chiefs"), not the city.
- **Big predictions.** "I think it'll be close" is plenty.

## The exit line

Leaving before the end? Read the score first.

> "Great game. I can't watch the ending, my heart can't take it."
